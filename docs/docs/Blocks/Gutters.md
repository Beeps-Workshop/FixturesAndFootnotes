# Gutters

Copper gutters catch the rain off your roof and carry it down to a basin or cauldron below.

![COPPER spelled out in copper gutters](/img/projects/fnf/gutters-copper.png)

A setup has three pieces:

- **Gutter**: an open channel along the edge of the roof.
- **Drain Pipe**: the pipe that carries the water down the wall.
- **Drain**: the spout at the bottom, where the water comes out.

## Setting one up
1. Place gutters along the edge of a roof. Click a wall to put a gutter against it, or click an
   existing gutter to continue the run. Gutters next to each other join up and turn corners on
   their own, like stairs.
2. Hang a drain pipe one block **down and one block out** from a gutter, against the wall, not
   directly underneath it. Stack more pipe down the wall from there.
3. Put a drain at the bottom of the pipe. Its spout points toward you.
4. Put a basin or a cauldron under the spout.

:::note
Corners don't connect in every arrangement yet. A plain L of two gutters, or gutters on two walls
that meet at the same corner of a building, may stay straight instead of turning.
:::

![A gutter along a porch roof, draining down a pipe into a basin](/img/projects/fnf/gutters-setup.png)

## Rain
While it's raining, water streams out of the drain, as long as part of the gutter run is open to
the sky. The gutter right above the pipe can be under an overhang. When the rain stops, the drain
keeps dripping for a minute or so.

The water lands just in front of the spout and falls straight down into whatever is below:

- A **basin** or **water cauldron** gains a layer about every twenty seconds, so it fills up in
  about a minute of steady rain.
- An **empty cauldron** starts filling with water.
- Lava and powder snow cauldrons are left alone.

![A drain pouring rainwater into a basin](/img/projects/fnf/gutters-rain.png)

## Basin
The basin is a shallow copper wash basin that only holds water. It works like a cauldron: fill and
empty it with buckets and bottles, and use it to wash leather armour, banners and shulker boxes.
It also fills slowly in the rain on its own, but dripstone won't fill it.

## Weathering
Gutters, pipes, drains and basins all weather like other copper blocks. Wax them with
**honeycomb** to stop that, or scrape them back with an **axe**.

## Crafting
Everything is cut from copper blocks on a **stonecutter**. Each weathering stage, waxed or not,
cuts into its own pieces.

| Piece      | Per copper block |
|------------|------------------|
| Gutter     | 8                |
| Drain Pipe | 8                |
| Drain      | 4                |
| Basin      | 1                |

The basin can also be crafted from five copper ingots.
